# Slides outline (7 slides, English)

Headline finding, used throughout: **EGIZ finds Earth's closest matches to Moon and Mars sites — and measures how far to trust them. Kazakhstan's Mangystau ranks in the top 1 % for Jezero, but the honest test shows the method mostly finds "bare, dry land", so these are field-check leads, not proven analogs.**

All numbers from `docs/discovery/RESULTS.md` (generated by code).

1. **Title.** EGIZ — Where on Earth is the Moon or Mars, and where does the analogy break? Team from Astana · NASA Space Apps 2026 · Challenge: Identify Earth locations that analog the permanent Moon base locations and Mars.

2. **The problem.** Analog sites are chosen by tradition and logistics. Nobody can say, for a specific target, which places on all of Earth are closest — and what no place on Earth can teach. Visual: map of past analog campaigns vs. an empty "rest of Earth".

3. **What EGIZ does.** 4,544 measured Earth windows × 10 targets (6 lunar, 4 Mars) × 8 measured dimensions with 90 % intervals; 4 dimensions shown as "not assessed". NASA data: LOLA, MOLA, POWER, HiRISE, SPICE; ESA: Copernicus DEM, WorldCover. Visual: Discover screen (target → map → shortlist).

4. **A candidate dossier.** Jezero → rank 1 window in western China, fidelity 0.63 [0.42–0.96]. Gap ledger: gravity ×2.6, pressure, radiation, day length; rover slope limit and sinkage are body-level model estimates. "Test it in Mission Lab". Visual: dossier and gap ledger.

5. **Kazakhstan, zoomed in.** Mangystau window: rank 43 of 4,544 for Jezero (99.0th percentile); Boszhira benchmark at the 97.3rd. 144 refinement windows at 20 km: a neighbour beats its seed for 14 of 18 seeds; best would-be rank 25. Caveat on the slide: best-of-many favours chance highs. Visual: `#/discover?region=kz`.

6. **How far to trust it.** AUC vs. typical land 0.64–0.91; vs. bare, arid, latitude-matched land 0.21–0.74; recall@50 ≤ 0.1. Every lunar rank 1 is "closest found — weak match". Missing: mineralogy, dust, ice, caves (need NASA EMIT / Earthdata access). Visual: trust card.

7. **What's next.** EMIT mineralogy and MODIS land-surface temperature once Earthdata access is granted; field checks in Mangystau (slope survey, XRF, temperature loggers); an operational axis (access, permits). Repository, demo link, licence (MIT).
